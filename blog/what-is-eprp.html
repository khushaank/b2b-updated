What Is EPRP? Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan Explained

Understand what an EPRP is, what it should contain, how emergency roles and drills work, and which Indian requirements may apply.

EPRP stands for Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan. It explains how an organisation will prepare for, respond to and recover from foreseeable emergencies.

Purpose and contents of an EPRP

An EPRP converts emergency risks into clear actions. It should define who raises the alarm, who takes command, how people evacuate or shelter, where they assemble, how headcounts work, who contacts external responders, and how the organisation returns to safe operation.

A useful plan normally includes emergency contacts, site drawings, escape routes, assembly points, shutdown responsibilities, first-aid arrangements, communication methods, resources, vulnerable-person assistance, contractor and visitor controls, and records of training and drills.

Roles, alarms and communication

Name the incident controller, site controller, wardens, first aiders, fire team, security and communication contacts. Give deputies for critical roles. Alarm signals should be recognisable, audible or visible where needed, supported by a backup method, and explained during induction.

Emergency scenarios and response

  • Fire: raise the alarm, call responders, isolate energy where safe, evacuate and account for people.
  • Chemical spill: identify the substance, isolate the area, use the safety data sheet, prevent incompatible contact and involve trained responders.
  • Medical emergency: contact trained first aiders and emergency medical services, control the scene and preserve access.
  • Natural hazard: define location-specific actions for flooding, earthquake, severe weather or another credible event.

The plan must reflect the site's real inventory, occupancy, layout, neighbours, utilities and external response times. One generic plan cannot safely cover every facility.

Which Indian law requires an EPRP?

No single Indian law universally requires the same document called an “EPRP” for every business. Duties depend on the establishment, hazardous processes and substances, factory and state rules, environmental approvals, fire authority conditions, disaster-management arrangements, and petroleum or chemical storage requirements.

For applicable hazardous industrial activities, Rule 13 of the Manufacture, Storage and Import of Hazardous Chemicals Rules, 1989 addresses preparation and updating of an on-site emergency plan. The Factories Act, 1948 includes provisions on fire precautions and hazardous processes, with operational requirements also shaped by applicable state rules. Environmental consent conditions, fire NOC conditions and sector-specific approvals may add site-specific duties.

Source review date: July 28, 2026. This is general information, not legal advice. Verify the current central and state requirements, approval conditions and local authority directions with the relevant authority or a qualified professional.

Training, drills and improvement

Train people for their actual roles and keep attendance and competency records. Conduct mock drills at a frequency based on applicable requirements and risk. Test a clear objective—such as alarm recognition, spill isolation or headcount accuracy—rather than treating evacuation time as the only measure.

After every drill or real event, record observations, causes, actions, owners and due dates. Update drawings, contacts, resources and procedures when the facility changes.

Practical EPRP example

A warehouse storing flammable liquids models a drum leak and ignition. The plan assigns alarm and isolation duties, identifies compatible spill materials, controls ignition sources, sets an upwind assembly point, provides the fire service with the inventory and drawing, and defines how wardens account for employees, contractors and visitors. A drill reveals that one loading-bay alarm is hard to hear, so the site adds a visual beacon and retests it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the full form of EPRP?

EPRP stands for Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan.

Does every Indian business need the same EPRP?

No. Applicable duties and the required plan depend on the establishment, hazards, state rules, approvals and sector-specific requirements.

How often should an EPRP be tested?

Use the frequency required by applicable rules and approvals and justified by site risk; also retest after significant changes or lessons from an event.
